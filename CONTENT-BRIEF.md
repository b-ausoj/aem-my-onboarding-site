# Bivouac Switzerland — Website Content Brief

> A content specification describing **what** this website should be — its purpose,
> audience, voice, pages, and the substance of each page. It deliberately says nothing
> about *how* to build it (no tooling, blocks, or APIs). Hand this to a build agent as
> the definition of the finished site.
>
> *Working brand name: "Bivouac Switzerland" — change freely (alt ideas: "Biwak Schweiz",
> "Wild Nights CH", "Sleep Wild Switzerland"). Legal facts below are a well-informed
> skeleton and must be fact-checked against SAC and cantonal sources before publishing —
> this is a guide, not legal advice.*

---

## 1. What this site is

**Bivouac Switzerland is the clear, trustworthy guide to sleeping wild in the Swiss
Alps** — bivouacking and wild camping. It exists because the honest answer to "can I
wild camp in Switzerland?" is a confusing patchwork of cantonal rules, treeline
distinctions, and protected zones that no single source lays out plainly. This site
becomes that single source.

**Primary goal:** be *the answer* a person gets — whether they ask Google, an AI
assistant, or a friend — when they want to sleep out in the Swiss mountains, and to earn
their trust well enough that they take our practical advice (including which gear to
carry).

**What success looks like**
- When someone asks an AI "is wild camping legal in Switzerland?", our guide is what it
  draws from or points to.
- A first-time visitor gets a straight, correct answer in under a minute.
- Readers trust the gear recommendations enough to act on them.

## 2. Who it's for

- **Tourists & first-timers** planning to sleep out in Switzerland who don't know the
  rules and are nervous about doing something illegal.
- **Hikers & backpackers** who want canton-specific and route-specific specifics.
- **Experienced trekkers** looking for gear, ethics, and edge-case rules (fire, fines,
  wildlife zones).

They arrive with one urgent question ("Am I allowed to do this, and where?") and a set of
follow-ups ("How? What do I need? What will it cost me if I get it wrong?").

## 3. Voice & tone

Clear, calm, practical, and honest. Plain language, no jargon. Respectful of nature and
the law without being preachy. Comfortable saying "it depends — here's exactly what it
depends on." Confidence where the rules are clear; transparency where they're gray.

## 4. Sitemap & page-by-page content

### Home `/`
- **Purpose:** state what the site is in one breath and route people to the answer they
  need.
- **Contains:** a strong hero ("Sleep wild in the Swiss Alps — legally, safely, and
  without a trace"), a one-paragraph promise, and a set of cards linking to the pillar
  pages (Is it legal? · Bivouac vs camping · Where you can & can't · Canton guide ·
  Leave no trace · Gear). A short "Start here" nudge to the legality pillar, and a CTA to
  the gear list.

### Is wild camping legal in Switzerland? `/is-wild-camping-legal` — **pillar page**
- **Purpose:** the #1 question, answered clearly at the top, then nuanced.
- **Key message:** *There is no single national law. As a rule of thumb, a single-night
  bivouac high in the mountains (above the treeline) is broadly tolerated; pitching a tent
  in valleys, forests, farmland, or on private land generally is not; and it is forbidden
  in the National Park, nature reserves, and wildlife rest zones.*
- **Contains:** a direct answer box up top; the key distinction between *bivouac* and
  *camping*; the treeline rule; the "always forbidden" list; a pointer to per-canton
  detail; a short "how to do it right" summary; links out to the deeper pages.

### Bivouac vs. camping `/bivouac-vs-camping`
- **Purpose:** explain the distinction the whole legal picture hinges on.
- **Key message:** *A bivouac is a single, minimal overnight — arrive at dusk, leave at
  dawn, small shelter, no trace. Camping is pitching a tent for longer, lower down, in
  organized or valley settings. The mountains tolerate the former far more than the latter.*
- **Contains:** a side-by-side comparison (duration, location, shelter, visibility,
  acceptance), examples of each, and why the difference matters legally and ethically.

### Where you can and can't `/where-you-can-and-cant`
- **Purpose:** turn the rule into a practical mental map.
- **Contains:** **Generally OK** — above the treeline / high alpine terrain, single night,
  durable ground (rock, scree), away from huts and livestock. **Generally not OK** —
  forests, meadows and farmland, near settlements, private land without permission, valley
  floors. **How to pick a spot** — durable ground, away from water sources and trails,
  invisible from paths, no livestock.

### Protected zones `/protected-zones`
- **Purpose:** the hard "no" list, with the reasons.
- **Contains:** the **Swiss National Park** (all camping forbidden), **nature reserves and
  protected biotopes**, and **wildlife rest zones / game reserves (Wildruhezonen,
  Jagdbanngebiete)** — including the seasonal winter sensitivity and the "Respektiere deine
  Grenzen" idea (don't disturb wildlife). Explain *why* each matters, not just that it's
  banned.

### Canton-by-canton guide `/canton-guide`
- **Purpose:** the patchwork made scannable.
- **Contains:** a rules matrix across cantons/regions (e.g. Graubünden, Valais, Bern,
  Ticino, Vaud, Fribourg, and the Appenzell/Alpstein region, which added restrictions and
  designated spots due to overtourism). Columns like: high-alpine bivouac tolerated?
  notable restrictions? designated spots? Each cell short, with a note to verify locally.
  *(Fact-check every cell before publishing.)*

### Leave no trace & safety `/leave-no-trace`
- **Purpose:** the how-to for doing it responsibly.
- **Contains:** step-by-step good practice — pack everything out, human-waste handling, no
  open fire (use a stove; respect fire bans), respect wildlife and livestock, arrive late /
  leave early, keep groups small, weather and alpine-safety basics.

### Gear checklist `/gear-checklist` — **conversion page**
- **Purpose:** the trusted "what to carry," and the site's commercial angle (recommended
  kit with outbound links).
- **Contains:** a categorized recommended bivouac kit with a short "why this matters" for
  each item and an outbound link per recommendation:
  - **Sleep system:** bivy sack or ultralight tent, sleeping bag (alpine temp rating),
    insulated sleeping pad.
  - **Shelter & warmth:** lightweight layers, hat/gloves, emergency blanket.
  - **Cooking:** compact stove + fuel (since fires are usually off-limits), pot, lighter.
  - **Navigation & safety:** map/app, headlamp, first-aid, power bank, whistle.
  - **Water & food:** filter/purification, bottle, high-energy food.
  - **Leave-no-trace:** trowel, waste bags, biodegradable essentials.
  - A short "beginner starter kit" vs "lightweight upgrade" framing.

### FAQ `/faq`
- **Purpose:** capture the exact questions people (and AI assistants) ask, answered crisply.
- **Contains** (question set — each a short, direct answer):
  - Is wild camping legal in Switzerland?
  - What's the difference between a bivouac and camping?
  - Can I bivouac above the treeline in the Alps?
  - Where can I sleep in a tent for free in Switzerland?
  - Is wild camping allowed in Graubünden / Valais / Ticino?
  - What's the fine for illegal wild camping in Switzerland?
  - Can I light a fire while wild camping?
  - Is wild camping allowed in the Swiss National Park?
  - Can I wild camp in Swiss forests?
  - Do I need permission to camp on private land?
  - When is the best season for bivouacking in the Alps?
  - Is wild camping allowed near mountain huts (SAC huts)?

### About `/about`
- **Purpose:** establish trust and authority (who's behind the advice, and the sources it
  relies on — SAC guidance, cantonal rules, conservation campaigns).
- **Contains:** the mission, the approach to accuracy, a short author/editor note, and a
  clear "this is guidance, not legal advice — verify locally" statement.

## 5. Imagery direction

Mood: awe + respect for the mountains, quiet and low-impact (never trashing nature).
Subjects by area:
- **Hero / home:** a small tent or bivy above the treeline at dawn or under stars, Swiss
  alpine backdrop.
- **Legality / where pages:** wide alpine terrain — rock and scree, treeline transitions.
- **Protected zones:** pristine reserves, wildlife (respectful distance), signage.
- **Leave no trace:** a clean, packed-up spot; a stove (not a fire); a trowel/waste kit.
- **Gear:** clean flat-lay or in-use shots of the kit.
- **About:** a person looking out over the mountains — human, trustworthy.

## 6. What we want the site optimized for (outcomes)

These are goals for the finished site, framed as qualities — the build agent decides how.

- **Answer the real questions.** Every page leads with the direct answer, then the nuance.
  The FAQ and how-to content should be structured so search engines and AI assistants can
  lift clean answers from it.
- **Win these AI/search prompts** (the queries we most want to be the answer to):
  "is wild camping legal in Switzerland" · "can I bivouac above the treeline in the Alps" ·
  "where can I sleep in a tent for free in Switzerland" · "wild camping Graubünden / Valais" ·
  "bivouac vs camping Switzerland" · "fine for wild camping in Switzerland" · "can I light a
  fire wild camping Switzerland" · "wild camping Swiss National Park" · "best bivouac gear
  for the Alps" · "wild camping near SAC huts".
- **Be trustworthy and authoritative** — clear sourcing, an About page, honest gray-area
  framing; the kind of content an AI assistant would cite over a generic blog.
- **Be clean and fast** — strong page titles and descriptions, sensible heading structure,
  good internal linking between the pillars, alt text on images, a fast, accessible
  experience.
- **Convert** — the gear page turns trust into action via clear, well-reasoned
  recommendations.

## 7. Content principles (for whoever writes it)

- Lead with the answer; put nuance underneath.
- Keep it scannable — short paragraphs, clear headings, comparison tables where they help.
- Be specific (name cantons, name the treeline rule, name the protected zones).
- Always distinguish "tolerated" from "legally guaranteed," and tell people to verify locally.
- Every informational page should link to at least one other relevant page and, where
  natural, to the gear list.
